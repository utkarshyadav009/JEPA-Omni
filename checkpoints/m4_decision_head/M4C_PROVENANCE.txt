── Wiring ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Each tick: refresh perception (M2 World-State for AV content via
encode_world_state, Whisper speech-activity feature via mean-pooled hidden
states) -> decision head -> on SPEAK, build the soft prompt from whichever
connector(s) are live (M3 for AV, M4b for speech) + frozen LLM, generate
TOKEN-BY-TOKEN with a manual KV-cache loop (models/m4_duplex_loop.py
DuplexLoop.generate_interruptible) so an external interrupt can halt
between tokens -- the standard all-at-once .generate() call cannot be
stopped early, so a custom loop was necessary to make "interruption
latency" a real, measurable quantity rather than an assumption. The
perceptual soft prompt refreshes independently of generation history, per
the M4a design -- callers control the refresh cadence separately from how
far along a generation is.

── Shift-trigger metric — selected on real data ────────────────────────
scripts/m4_shift_metric_easycom.py: real graded turn transitions (4,168
consecutive EasyCom segment pairs, boundary=genuine speaker change, scored
in Whisper feature space since World-State doesn't exist for EasyCom).
  euclidean:    AUC=0.614  Cohen's d=0.400
  cosine:       AUC=0.612  Cohen's d=0.363
  mahalanobis:  AUC=0.497  Cohen's d=-0.004  (CHANCE LEVEL)
The previous default (Mahalanobis, justified by M2 World-State's measured
anisotropy, effective_rank=37.71/1024) made a testable prediction and the
test failed it -- whitening likely dilutes the speaker-identity signal
that lives in Whisper's natural high-variance directions, which Euclidean
already exploits fine. Default flipped to euclidean in models/
m4_shift_trigger.py. AUC=0.614 is real but modest -- mean-pooled utterance
embeddings discard most speaker-specific acoustic detail; a dedicated
speaker embedding would likely do better, out of scope here. Given the
decision head is already cheap (1.32M-param MLP, no LLM), the shift
trigger's original motivation (avoid expensive recomputation) is much less
load-bearing than when first designed for gating LLM calls directly --
kept implemented and selectable for future use, not currently required by
the core M4c loop.

── KNOWN LIMITATION (flagged explicitly) ────────────────────────────────
All EasyCom data (train AND held-out test) comes from 7 speakers / 12
sessions. Checked directly: the 5 speaker IDs in the held-out test sessions
{3,4,5,6,7} are the EXACT SAME 5 IDs present in the train sessions -- ZERO
unseen speakers in the evaluation. "Held-out session" means held-out
recording, not held-out voice. Generalization to unseen speakers, accents,
or rooms is completely untested. The 0.988 F1 should not be read as a
realistic deployment-scale generalization number.
